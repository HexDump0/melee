## Read this before quoting the coverage number

**Coverage counts descriptors reached, not bytes the game reads.** This session
saw it move in every direction for the wrong reasons, so treat it as a ratchet
against regression, not as a measure of progress:

- **v106 lowered it by 14** and that was a *fix* — it removed a wrong walk.
- **v107 (P-778) did not move it at all** and fixed a crash in every match with
  a Party Ball. A chain fix shows up in the soak, not in the metric.
- **v114 raised it 2.9%, and 54% of that gain is data the engine never reads**
  — the unreachable ancestor prefix of the `PlXx` part-animation trees.

**Four separate "biggest target on the disc" figures turned out to be
measurement artifacts, not data.** In order: the `Vi*` motion-file overrun
(13,892 → 1,262 changeable words), `ItCo` 0x4ec8 (an array already walked whose
base was never `mark()`ed, 2,368 → 0), the `PlXx` nodes `ftAnim_GetNextAnimJointInTree`
cannot reach (16,025), and `cold` counting palindromic padding (75% of it
disc-wide). The honest disc-wide figure went from **"197,138 words"** to about
**10,400 changeable**, most of which should not be walked.

`MELEE_UNWALKED` now prints **`chg`** — cold words a byte swap would actually
change — and its extent stops at public symbols. Sort by `chg`, never `cold`.

## What I tried that did not work

- **`HSD_MAX_DEPTH` is not truncating anything.** Raised it to 4096: coverage
  did not move one descriptor. Do not spend time here.
- **P-771 was not the `Pl*` descriptor gap**, which the P-758 row predicted.
  Stage 7 failed identically before and after the head item.
- **A shape heuristic does not work on `hat_dynamics[]`**, and I nearly shipped
  one. The slots are overloaded per fighter: `[1]` is a `u32` mask and not a
  pointer at all. Same trap as G&W's `x48_items[10]` (P-765).
- **I shipped a bug and caught it myself (v105).** The part-animation array was
  bounded by its relocation run and by `next_pointed_at_after`; neither stops
  at an object the game reaches **by name**, and `ftData` is exactly that. It
  corrupted 20 words across Falcon, DK and both wireframes, including
  public-symbol name strings, and **no gate caught it** — ctest and the soak
  were identical. I found it by diffing converted bytes across all 861
  archives. `next_public_after` is now the third bound.

> **If you bound an array by relocation evidence, ask what the next struct
> field after the array is. If it is a pointer, your bound does not exist.**

That one sentence would have prevented v105, the `ItCo` miscount and the `Vi*`
miscount.
